About

An investigative journalist for over two decades, recognised with the International Press Institute (Vienna) Award for Excellence in Journalism (2011) for Rent a Riot, published in Tehelka, which exposed a plan to organise communal violence for money. He was a member of the Foreign Press Association, London (2018–2023).

His best-known investigation is Cobrapost's Operation 136 (2018) — a landmark undercover exposé of paid news, described as India's equivalent of the Cambridge Analytica revelations. Working under an alias, he approached the owners and senior management of more than three dozen media houses and documented their willingness to run a paid political and communal campaign ahead of the 2019 general election in exchange for money. Cobrapost released the series in two parts, on 26 March and 25 May 2018; several of the named houses denied the allegations.

Among his other investigations — as recited in his own submissions on the record of the High Court of Delhi (CRL.M.C. 5691/2022) — are Sick Man Walking (Tehelka, 2010), on the Satyam scam, in which he secured hidden-camera footage later relied upon by the CBI in the trial of B. Ramalinga Raju; If the Pose Holds (Outlook–IBN7, October 2012), on the disappearance of Swami Shankardev, the guru of Baba Ramdev, after which the Government ordered a CBI inquiry into the affair (CBI Case No. RC 2(S)/2013); and a 2018 investigation into PMO involvement in Paytm's data-sharing practices around demonetisation. His stories and investigative footage have also been aired internationally, including on Al Jazeera English.

A law graduate, he worked in London between 2019 and 2022 on a contractual basis with law firms on criminal and asylum matters — drafting, case strategy and litigation support. That work is reflected in United Kingdom Ministry of Justice records and in the extradition judgment of District Judge Samuel Goozée in the Nirav Modi proceedings (Westminster Magistrates' Court, 25 February 2021). He is currently pursuing an LL.M.

He now appears as Petitioner-in-Person before the High Court of Delhi, the High Court of Punjab & Haryana, and the Supreme Court of India. Of the many proceedings arising from his journalism, the large majority have concluded; the matters set out below are those that remain live, together with two constitutional public-interest petitions.
